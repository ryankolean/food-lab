# Bulgogi Marinade

Maangchi's bulgogi: thinly sliced beef in a sweet-savory marinade of crushed Asian pear, onion purée, garlic,
soy sauce and sesame oil. Maangchi (Emily Kim) is the best-known teacher of Korean home cooking in English,
through her YouTube channel, website and the books *Maangchi's Real Korean Cooking* and *Maangchi's Big Book
of Korean Cooking*. The pear both sweetens and tenderizes the beef.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~120 g | Asian (Korean) pear | ½ cup crushed. Peel, core and rough-chop. Or 1 small Bosc pear |
| ~60 g | Onion | ¼ cup purée; rough-chopped |
| 4 cloves | Garlic | Peeled; ~20 g |
| ~5 g | Fresh ginger | 1 teaspoon minced; peeled coin |
| 1 | Green onion | Rough-chopped; ~15 g |
| ~36 g | Soy sauce | 2 tablespoons |
| ~26 g | Brown sugar | 2 tablespoons (or 1 tablespoon brown sugar + 1½ tablespoons rice syrup) |
| ~13 g | Toasted sesame oil | 1 tablespoon |
| Pinch | Ground black pepper | |
| ~450 g | Beef tenderloin, rib-eye or top sirloin | 1 lb, sliced paper-thin against the grain |

## Method

1. Into the deli container: soy sauce, sesame oil and brown sugar, then the pear, onion, garlic and ginger.
   Blend smooth with the stick blender, head submerged.
2. Add the green onion and pepper and pulse a few times so the green onion stays in small pieces.
3. Mix the marinade into the beef. Marinate in the fridge 30 minutes to a few hours, no longer than
   overnight (window is an estimate; see Notes).
4. To cook: get a cast-iron pan or grill pan very hot. Cook the beef in batches without crowding, tossing,
   until browned and just cooked, 2–4 minutes. Partly freeze the beef for an hour before slicing it if
   you're cutting it yourself.

## To serve

Over rice, or wrapped in lettuce with rice, ssamjang and kimchi. Garnish with sesame seeds and sliced green
onion.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** The marinade alone keeps 2–3 days (estimate). Marinated raw beef: cook within 24 hours.
- **Freezer:** Untested. Freezing marinated bulgogi is common in Korean home cooking, but **flag it**:
  Asian pear contains a protein-breaking enzyme, and paper-thin beef is very exposed to it. A long fridge
  soak plus a slow thaw can turn the beef mealy. Freeze it quickly after mixing, in flat bags, and thaw in
  the fridge right before cooking. Compare against a fresh batch.

## Notes

### From the source
- If you can't find an Asian pear, 1 small Bosc pear per pound of beef works.
- Maangchi also has an "easy bulgogi" with no pear or onion (water, sugar, soy, garlic, green onion,
  sesame oil) and a spicy version with gochugaru and gochujang.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** The source crushes the pear and purées the onion separately and minces the
  garlic and ginger. Here they're blended together in one container.
- **Scale up:** this is a small batch. Double or triple it for meal prep; it still fits a quart container.
- **Marinating window** is an estimate, not from the source.
- **Gram estimates** for the pear, onion, garlic and ginger are approximate. Maangchi's page couldn't be
  opened from this session; the quantities came via search results.
- **Still to check on maangchi.com:** pear, onion, garlic and ginger amounts match search snippets of this
  recipe, but the soy sauce, brown sugar/rice syrup and sesame oil amounts only matched her easy and pork
  bulgogi pages. The page blocked fetching.
