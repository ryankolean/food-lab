# Pok Pok Kai Yaang (Gai Yang) Marinade

Thai grilled chicken from Andy Ricker, whose Portland restaurant Pok Pok (and its cookbook) brought
northern Thai street food to the US and won him a James Beard award. Kai yaang was the dish Pok Pok was
built around. The flavor is garlic, white pepper, cilantro stem, lemongrass and ginger, seasoned with fish
sauce and thin soy sauce. Small birds are the point: they cook through over charcoal before the skin burns.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~36 g | Thai fish sauce | 2 tablespoons |
| ~36 g | Thai thin soy sauce | 2 tablespoons (e.g., Healthy Boy) |
| ~30 g | Water | 2 tablespoons |
| ~2 g | Sugar | ½ teaspoon |
| ~0.5 g | Ground black pepper | ¼ teaspoon |
| 5–6 cloves | Garlic | Unpeeled in the source's brine; peel here; ~25–30 g |
| ~3 g | White peppercorns | 1 teaspoon |
| 1 thumb-size piece | Fresh ginger | Unpeeled in the source's brine; peel here, slice into coins; ~15 g |
| 1 large stalk | Lemongrass | Tender lower third only, thin-sliced crosswise; ~15 g |
| 1 small handful | Cilantro stems (and roots if you can get them) | ~15 g |
| 2–3 | Green onions | Rough-chopped; ~30–45 g |
| ~1.1–1.4 kg | Cornish hens or poussins | 2 birds, 1¼–1½ lb each, spatchcocked |

## Method

1. Toast the white peppercorns in a dry pan until fragrant, about 1 minute. Grind them.
2. Into the deli container: fish sauce, soy sauce, water, sugar and both peppers, then the garlic,
   ginger, lemongrass, cilantro stems and green onions. Blend to a loose, slightly gritty paste with the stick blender,
   head submerged, working the lemongrass for a full minute.
3. Salt the birds lightly all over (see Notes; this stands in for the source's brine) and rub the paste
   over them and under the skin. Marinate uncovered in the fridge at least 4 hours, preferably overnight.
4. To cook (source): set up a covered charcoal grill for indirect heat at 350–375°F / 175–190°C. Cook the
   birds breast-up away from the coals, lid vents over the birds, 25 minutes. Flip to brown the other
   side, flip back after about 5 minutes, then move them over the coals and keep turning until the skin is
   glossy golden brown and the thigh juices run clear, 5–10 minutes more. Oven alternative: 350°F / 175°C
   on a rack for 30 minutes, then 400°F / 200°C, checking every 5 minutes. Rest and chop through the bone.

## To serve

With sticky rice, green papaya salad (som tam) and a dipping sauce: jaew (tamarind and toasted chile) or
sweet chile sauce.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** The paste keeps about 3–4 days (estimate).
- **Freezer:** Untested. Good candidate: no citrus or fruit, so raw chicken frozen in it shouldn't go mushy.
  The fish sauce and soy act like a slow brine; watch that the result isn't too salty after a long freeze.

## Notes

### From the source
- Ricker's kai yaang is brined first: ½ cup kosher salt and ¼ cup superfine sugar in 10 cups tepid water,
  with the unpeeled garlic, white peppercorns, unpeeled ginger (sliced), lemongrass (in 2-inch lengths),
  cilantro stems and 2–3 green onions (torn) added to the brine. Brine at least 4 hours, preferably
  overnight.
- The birds then air-dry uncovered in the fridge 4–12 hours, are brushed with the fish sauce–soy marinade
  and rest 2 hours before grilling over indirect heat.
- On the grill they're brushed with shallot oil and then a honey mixture (those recipes aren't copied here).

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick-blender adaptation (the big change).** A 10-cup brine doesn't fit a quart container, so the brine
  aromatics are blended straight into the marinade instead (as Ricker's Saveur version reportedly does), and a
  light dry salt (~1% of the chicken weight, ~12 g Diamond kosher for 1.25 kg) replaces the brine.
- **Lemongrass** is fibrous. Slice it thin crosswise before blending; strain or wipe off stringy bits.
- In Ricker's Saveur gai yahng, about ten whole garlic cloves, cilantro, soy sauce, oil and white pepper
  are reportedly blended into a marinade, with some held back for basting. Saveur blocked fetching, so
  this is unconfirmed.
- **Marinating window** for the dry-salted, paste-rubbed birds is an estimate based on the source's brine time.
- **Gram estimates** throughout are approximate.
- Quantities and method were read from the Philadelphia Inquirer reprint of the Pok Pok recipe, not the
  book.
