# Jamaican Jerk Marinade

The wet jerk paste from the Jerk Shack, the open-air jerk stand at Sandals' Jamaican resorts, which Sandals
publishes as its "secret" recipe. It's a green, scallion-heavy paste: scotch bonnet for heat, allspice
(pimento) for the warm, clove-like backbone, fresh thyme, ginger and onion, loosened with soy sauce and oil.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~120 g | Scallions | 1½ cups, white and green parts, rough-chopped |
| ~110 g | Onion | About ¾ cup, rough-chopped |
| 1 tablespoon | Scotch bonnet | Source: 1 tablespoon finely chopped (~1 pepper, ~10 g). Stem it; leave seeds in for heat |
| ~5 g | Fresh thyme leaves | 2 tablespoons |
| ~7 g | Ground allspice | 1 tablespoon |
| ~12 g | Fresh ginger | 2 tablespoons; peeled, sliced into coins |
| ~10 g | Garlic | 1 tablespoon, about 2 cloves, peeled |
| ~1 g | Ground black pepper | ½ teaspoon |
| ~70 g | Soy sauce | **Estimate**, ~¼ cup. The amount is garbled in every copy found |
| ~55 g | Vegetable oil | **Estimate**, ~¼ cup. The amount is garbled in every copy found |
| ~1.5–2 kg | Bone-in chicken thighs and legs | Or quarters. Slash to the bone 2–3 times |

## Method

1. If you have whole allspice berries, toast ~2 tablespoons in a dry pan until fragrant, then grind them
   for the 1 tablespoon of ground allspice.
2. Into the deli container: soy sauce and oil first, then onion, ginger, garlic, scotch bonnet, thyme,
   allspice and pepper, with the scallions on top. Blend to a smooth, pourable paste with the stick
   blender, head submerged, pushing the scallions down as they break up.
3. Wear gloves. Rub the paste into the chicken and into the slashes. Marinate in the fridge 12–24 hours
   (estimate; see Notes).
4. To cook: grill over a medium two-zone charcoal fire, skin side up on the cool side, lid on, until the
   thighs reach ~175°F / 80°C, 35–45 minutes. Finish skin-side down over the coals to char. Ideally add
   soaked allspice wood or a handful of whole allspice berries to the coals for smoke.

## To serve

Chopped into pieces with rice and peas, festival or hard dough bread, and a little extra paste or hot
sauce on the side.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** The paste keeps about a week (estimate; jerk pastes are commonly kept that long).
- **Freezer:** Untested. Good candidate. The paste has no citrus or fruit, so freezing raw chicken in it
  shouldn't hurt the texture; the salt from the soy sauce works like a slow brine. Scallion color will
  dull.

## Notes

### From the source
- The source processes everything except the soy sauce and oil, then stirs those in.
- Scotch bonnet amount is given as 1 tablespoon finely chopped, which is mild for jerk. Add more to taste.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** Rough-chop instead of finely chop, and put the soy and oil in first so
  the blade has liquid to pull the scallions down.
- **Soy sauce and oil amounts are estimates.** Every copy of the recipe found showed a garbled fraction.
  Check the Sandals page and correct the table.
- **Still to check on sandals.com:** search summaries of the Sandals page give ¼ cup soy sauce (matches)
  but **¾ cup vegetable oil**, not ¼ cup, and say to marinate meat overnight. The page blocked fetching.
- **Optional:** Many Jamaican home recipes add lime juice or vinegar and brown sugar. Not in this source.
- **Marinating window** (12–24 hours) and the grilling method are not from the source.
- **Gram estimates** throughout are approximate. The source page couldn't be opened from this session;
  the quantities came via search results.
- Swap note: a named Jamaican chef's recipe with complete quantities couldn't be confirmed by search, so
  this uses the Jerk Shack (a Jamaican jerk stand) instead.
