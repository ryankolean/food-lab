# Pollo a la Brasa Marinade

Peruvian rotisserie chicken, from Ricardo Zarate's *The Fire of Peru*. Zarate is the Lima-born chef behind
Mo-Chica and Picca in Los Angeles, and a Food & Wine Best New Chef. His marinade is dark and savory: ají
panca (a smoky, fruity Peruvian red chile), a lot of garlic, rosemary, cumin, soy sauce and red wine vinegar.
He poaches the marinated chicken gently in bags before roasting, which keeps it juicy, so it suits a
sous-vide routine.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~70 g | Soy sauce | ¼ cup (Yamasa preferred) |
| ~60 g | Red wine vinegar | ¼ cup |
| ~110 g | Extra-virgin olive oil | ½ cup |
| ~35 g | Ají panca paste | 2 tablespoons |
| ~60 g | Garlic | ¼ cup puréed, about 12 cloves, peeled |
| ~4 g | Fresh rosemary leaves | 1½ tablespoons chopped; stripped from the stems |
| ~3 g | Ground cumin | 1½ teaspoons |
| ~2 g | Ground black pepper | 1 teaspoon |
| 1 | Whole chicken | ~1.6–1.8 kg (3½–4 lb), halved or spatchcocked |

## Method

1. Into the deli container: soy sauce, vinegar and olive oil, then the ají panca, garlic, rosemary, cumin
   and pepper. Blend smooth with the stick blender, head submerged, until the garlic and rosemary
   disappear and the marinade is emulsified.
2. Put the chicken halves in a zip-top or vacuum bag with the marinade. Squeeze out the air and refrigerate
   overnight (window is an estimate; see Notes).
3. To cook (source method): poach the chicken in its bag in barely simmering water until just cooked
   through, then roast or grill hot to brown and crisp the skin. Sous-vide version (added later): 150°F /
   65.5°C for 2–3 hours, then roast at 475°F / 245°C or grill skin-side down until deep brown.
4. Without the poach: roast at 425°F / 220°C until the thigh reaches 175°F / 80°C, 45–60 minutes, basting
   once with the bag juices early on.

## To serve

Quartered, with fries, a simple salad and ají verde (the Peruvian green sauce of jalapeño, cilantro and
mayonnaise) and ají amarillo sauce.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** The marinade keeps up to 5 days (per source).
- **Freezer:** Untested. Good candidate for freeze-in-marinade: the only acid is a little red wine vinegar,
  diluted by oil and soy, so texture damage should be minimal. It's also already in a bag, so it can go
  straight from freezer to sous-vide bath (add ~1 hour to the cook from frozen).

## Notes

### From the source
- Whisk the dry paste ingredients first, then stir in the liquids. Makes about 1¼ cups.
- The marinade can be used right away or refrigerated up to five days.
- The chicken halves are soaked in the marinade, then poached slowly in bags to mimic sous-vide, which keeps
  the meat moist.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** Instead of puréeing the garlic and chopping the rosemary separately, it all
  goes into the container whole and gets blended.
- **Marinating window, sous-vide temperature and roasting temperatures** are estimates, not from the
  source.
- **Ají panca paste** is sold in jars at Latin markets. To make it from dried ají panca, toast, soak in hot
  water 30 minutes and blend with a little of the water (~2 dried chiles per 2 tablespoons paste, estimate).
- **Gram estimates** throughout are approximate. The New Worlder page couldn't be opened from this session;
  the quantities came via search results.
- Swap note: the target was Kenji's Serious Eats Peruvian chicken, but its quantities couldn't be
  confirmed (reposts were inconsistent), so this uses Zarate, a Peruvian chef.
