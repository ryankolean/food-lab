# Marinades: One-Container, Stick-Blender Marinades

High-flavor marinades with almost no knife work. Every recipe here follows the same rule: **it has to be
makeable in one quart (32 oz) deli container with a stick (immersion) blender.** The only prep is peeling,
rough-chopping, and toasting, charring or soaking. No mincing, no fine dice. Everything goes into the
container and gets blended.

Each recipe comes from a cook, cookbook or restaurant known for it. The original source is cited in each
file's front matter, and anything changed to fit the deli-container method is kept under a clearly labeled
note.

## The method: shared rules for every marinade here

### Building it in the container

1. **Liquids first, then aromatics, soft herbs on top.** Juice, soy, vinegar, oil or yogurt go in first so
   the blade has something to pull against. Garlic, ginger, onion and chiles next. Leafy herbs and scallions
   last, pushed down as they break up.
2. **Rough-chop only.** Peel garlic, slice ginger into coins, quarter onions, strip herbs off woody stems,
   slice lemongrass thin crosswise (it's the one fibrous exception). The blender does the rest.
3. **Soak dried chiles.** Toast them for seconds until fragrant, then soak in hot water or simmer in the
   recipe's stock for 10–20 minutes. A stick blender can't break down dry chile skins.
4. **Toast whole spices, then grind them.** A stick blender won't grind whole cumin, coriander, allspice or
   peppercorns. Use a spice grinder or mortar, or buy ground.
5. **Keep the blade head submerged.** Pulse to break things down, then run steadily while moving the head up
   and down. Lifting it pulls in air and splatters. Oil-heavy marinades (mojo, pollo a la brasa) will
   emulsify; that's good.
6. **Let hot ingredients cool a little first.** Warm is fine; boiling-hot splatters.

### Marinating

7. **Salt level.** A marinade should taste too salty on its own: it's seasoning the meat, not being eaten as
   is. Soy sauce and fish sauce count as salt.
8. **Time windows by protein** (general guidance, not tested here):
   - Fish and shellfish: 30 minutes to 2 hours, except miso cures (1–3 days).
   - Thin-sliced beef or pork: 30 minutes to overnight.
   - Chicken pieces: 4–24 hours.
   - Steak (skirt, flank): 2–10 hours.
   - Pork shoulder and whole chickens: overnight to 24 hours.
9. **Don't over-marinate acidic or enzymatic marinades.** Citrus, vinegar and yogurt slowly firm and then
   soften the outside of the meat. Fresh pineapple, papaya, kiwi, Asian pear and ginger contain enzymes that
   break down protein and can turn meat mushy or mealy. Shorten the window for these, especially on thin
   cuts and chicken breast.
10. **Reserve before it touches raw meat.** If you want some as a sauce or for basting, pour it off into a
    clean jar first. Marinade that has held raw meat has to be boiled hard for a few minutes before it's
    used as a sauce.
11. **Wipe off excess before searing.** Wet, sugary marinades steam and burn. Wipe or scrape them off and
    pat dry for a better crust.

### Freezer meal prep (untested)

12. **Freezing raw protein in the marinade** for the sous-vide/freezer routine: put the protein and marinade
    in a flat zip-top or vacuum bag, press out the air, freeze flat. Thaw in the fridge or cook from frozen
    sous-vide. All of this is untested. Expected results:
    - **Likely good:** Nobu miso (fish), kai yaang, jerk, pollo a la brasa, al pastor (no fresh pineapple).
    - **Test carefully:** mojo and carne asada (citrus), tandoori (yogurt plus lemon), bulgogi (Asian pear on
      thin beef). Freeze these right after mixing rather than after a full marinade, and compare against a
      fresh batch.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Citrus and fresh-herb marinades are best within 2–3 days. Chile adobos and aromatic pastes
  keep about 5–7 days. The miso marinade keeps much longer. Each file gives its own estimate.
- **Freezer:** Untested. Marinades without fresh herbs, dairy or fruit should freeze well on their own
  (re-blend after thawing). Test the freeze-in-marinade results using the
  [freezer test protocol](../sauces/README.md#test-protocol).
