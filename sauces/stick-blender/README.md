# Stick-Blender Sauces

High-flavor sauces from around the Mediterranean, the Middle East and beyond, each made in **one quart (32
oz) deli container with a stick (immersion) blender.** Prep is limited to peeling, rough-chopping, toasting,
charring or soaking. No fine knife work and no food processor.

Each recipe comes from a chef, cookbook or restaurant known for that sauce. The original source is cited in
each file's front matter, and anything changed to fit the deli-container method is kept under a clearly
labeled note. Jira: HOUSE-75.

Chimichurri lives in [`../steak/chimichurri.md`](../steak/chimichurri.md) and isn't repeated here.

## The method: shared rules for every sauce here

### The container

1. **Use a quart (32 oz) deli container.** The blender head must reach the bottom flat, and the batch must
   be deep enough to **cover the blade guard** before you start. If a small batch sits too shallow, tilt the
   container so everything pools around the head, or double the batch.
2. **Leave headroom.** Keep the unblended ingredients under about three-quarters full. If a batch is bigger
   (romesco), blend in two passes.
3. **Wet things first.** Liquid, soft ingredients and oil go in the bottom, firm things on top, so the blade
   has something to pull against.

### Emulsions (toum, hollandaise)

4. **Start with the head flat on the bottom** over the base (garlic paste, yolks) and add fat slowly while it
   runs: drops or teaspoons for toum, a thin stream of very hot butter for hollandaise. Once it thickens and
   turns pale, the rest can go faster. **Slowly lift the head** as the volume grows, then push it back down.
5. **The "pour it all on top" trick** (all the oil on top, head at the bottom, then lift) works for egg-based
   emulsions. It does **not** work reliably for toum, which has no egg.
6. **Breaking and rescue.** If it turns greasy and thin, stop. In a clean container, start a new base (a
   fresh yolk with a teaspoon of hot water for hollandaise; 2 more cloves of garlic blended to a paste with
   salt for toum) and blend the broken sauce back in a spoonful at a time.

### Chiles, nuts and seeds

7. **Soak dried chiles** in hot water for 20–30 minutes after toasting. A stick blender can't break down dry
   chile skins. Use some of the soaking water to help the blend.
8. **Grind whole spices first.** A stick blender won't grind whole seeds; use a spice grinder or mortar, or
   ground spices.
9. **Nuts are the limit.** A stick blender can't grind whole nuts. Toast them, crush them small in a bag
   with a rolling pin, and blend a long time, or use toasted nut flour. Tahini and other ready-made nut and
   seed pastes are fine.

### Herb sauces

10. **Pulse, don't purée**, when the source wants a coarse sauce (schug, chermoula). Blend the garlic, chiles
    and spices with some of the oil first, then add the herbs.

### Seasoning

11. **Season after blending.** Taste, add salt or acid, rest 10–15 minutes, and taste again.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** most of these keep about 5 days; schug, harissa and toum keep for weeks under oil. Mayonnaise
  and egg sauces (ají verde, hollandaise) are best fresh.
- **Freezer:** untested. Chile, herb-and-oil and nut sauces (harissa, schug, chermoula, romesco, maybe
  tahini) are good candidates. **Emulsions won't freeze:** toum, hollandaise and the mayonnaise-based ají
  verde will break. Test using the [freezer test protocol](../README.md#test-protocol).
