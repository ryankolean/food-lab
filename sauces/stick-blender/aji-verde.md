# Ají Verde (Peruvian Green Sauce)

The creamy, spicy cilantro sauce served with Peruvian rotisserie chicken. This is Kenji López-Alt's Serious
Eats version: a blender sauce of jalapeños, ají amarillo paste, cilantro, garlic, mayonnaise and sour cream,
with a drizzle of olive oil at the end.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 3 | Jalapeños | Whole, roughly chopped; ~70 g. Seed for less heat |
| ~15 g | Ají amarillo paste | 1 tablespoon (15 ml). Optional |
| 28 g | Cilantro leaves | 1 cup (1 oz) |
| ~10 g | Garlic | 2 medium cloves, peeled |
| ~110 g | Mayonnaise | ½ cup (120 ml) |
| ~60 g | Sour cream | ¼ cup (60 ml) |
| ~10 g | Lime juice | 2 teaspoons (10 ml), from 1 lime |
| ~5 g | Distilled white vinegar | 1 teaspoon (5 ml) |
| ~25 g | Extra-virgin olive oil | 2 tablespoons (30 ml) |
| | Kosher salt and black pepper | To taste |

## Method

1. Put the mayonnaise, sour cream, lime juice, vinegar, garlic, jalapeños and ají amarillo paste (if using)
   in a quart deli container. Blend with the stick blender, head on the bottom, until the jalapeños are fully
   broken down.
2. Add the cilantro and blend until the sauce is smooth and bright green, scraping down the sides as needed.
3. With the blender running, slowly drizzle in the olive oil. Season with salt and pepper. It will be thin at
   first and thicken as it rests; refrigerate in a sealed container until you need it.

## To serve

With roast chicken, fries and steak. It thickens as it sits.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** About 4–5 days (estimate). Press plastic onto the surface to keep the color.
- **Freezer:** Not recommended. Mayonnaise and sour cream separate when thawed.

## Notes

### From the source
- Everything but the oil is blended smooth, then the oil goes in slowly with the blender running.
- The sauce is thin at first and thickens as it rests.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Read from a transcription, not the original.** The Serious Eats page is blocked from this session; the
  quantities come from an uncredited transcription in Serious Eats' format (umami.recipes). Check against the
  original if you can reach it.
- **The Once Upon a Chef adaptation** (credited to Kenji) differs: no ají amarillo and no vinegar,
  1 tablespoon lime juice instead of 2 teaspoons, 1 cup *packed* cilantro, ½ teaspoon salt and ⅛ teaspoon
  pepper. Its author says it keeps 4–5 days.
- Neither version has cheese; earlier drafts listed cotija, which has been dropped.
- Jalapeños stand in for the traditional Peruvian ají amarillo and huacatay (black mint), which are hard to
  find; the ají amarillo paste gets it closer. The seeds and ribs hold most of the heat.
- Peruvian pollo a la brasa spots such as Pio Pio in New York serve a sauce like this.
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** The liquids go in first so the blade has something to work in, and the
  cilantro goes in second.
- **Gram estimates** are approximate.
