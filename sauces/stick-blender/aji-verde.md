# Ají Verde (Peruvian Green Sauce)

The creamy, spicy cilantro sauce served with rotisserie chicken at Peruvian pollo a la brasa spots like Pio
Pio in New York. Kenji López-Alt's Serious Eats version is the one most American home cooks learned from: a
blender sauce of jalapeños, cilantro and mayonnaise.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 3 | Jalapeños | Stems pulled off, halved; ~70 g. Seed for less heat |
| ~25 g | Cilantro leaves | 1 cup packed; tender stems are fine |
| ~110 g | Mayonnaise | ½ cup |
| ~60 g | Sour cream | ¼ cup |
| ~15 g | Lime juice | 1 tablespoon |
| ~5 g | Garlic | 1 clove, peeled. **Quantity unconfirmed** |
| ~15–30 g | Cotija (or Parmesan) | Crumbled or broken up. **Quantity unconfirmed** |
| ~15 g | Ají amarillo paste | Optional. **Quantity unconfirmed** |
| ~25 g | Olive oil | 2 tablespoons. **Unconfirmed; may not be in Kenji's version** |
| | Salt and black pepper | To taste |

## Method

1. Put the jalapeños, garlic, lime juice, mayonnaise and sour cream (and ají amarillo paste and cheese, if
   using) in a quart deli container. Blend with the stick blender, head on the bottom, until the jalapeños
   are fully broken down.
2. Add the cilantro and blend until the sauce is smooth and bright green.
3. With the blender running, drizzle in the olive oil (if using). Season with salt and pepper.

## To serve

With roast chicken, fries and steak. It thickens as it sits.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** About 4–5 days (estimate). Press plastic onto the surface to keep the color.
- **Freezer:** Not recommended. Mayonnaise and sour cream separate when thawed.

## Notes

### From the source
- Jalapeños stand in for the traditional Peruvian ají amarillo and huacatay (black mint), which are hard to
  find. The ají amarillo paste gets it closer.
- The seeds and ribs hold most of the heat.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Only the jalapeño, cilantro, mayonnaise, sour cream and lime quantities are confirmed**, and only through
  an adaptation (Once Upon a Chef) that credits Kenji's Serious Eats recipe. Garlic, cheese, ají amarillo and
  olive oil appear in other Serious Eats–based adaptations but their amounts are estimates. Check the
  original Serious Eats page and correct the table.
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** Mayonnaise and sour cream go in first so the blade has something to work in.
- **Gram estimates** are approximate. The source page couldn't be opened from this session; quantities came
  via search.
