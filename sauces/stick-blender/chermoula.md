# Chermoula

The Moroccan herb, garlic and spice sauce used as both a marinade and a finishing sauce, especially for
fish. Paula Wolfert spent decades documenting Moroccan home cooking; her books, including *Couscous and Other
Good Food from Morocco* and *The Food of Morocco*, are the standard English-language references.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~40 g | Garlic | About 8 cloves, peeled |
| ~15 g | Flat-leaf parsley leaves | ½ cup; tender stems are fine |
| ~10 g | Cilantro leaves | ⅓ cup; tender stems are fine |
| 2 | Lemons, zest only | ~4 g. Use a Microplane |
| ~9 g | Sweet paprika | 4 teaspoons. Moroccan paprika if you can get it |
| ~5 g | Chile powder | 2 teaspoons. Or a pinch of cayenne for more heat |
| ~4 g | Ground cumin | 2 teaspoons |
| ~215 g | Olive oil | 1 cup |
| ~4 g | Salt | **Estimate**; to taste |
| | Lemon juice | Optional, to taste (see Notes) |

## Method

1. Put the garlic, salt, cumin, paprika, chile powder, lemon zest and about ¼ cup of the oil in a quart deli
   container. Blend with the stick blender, head on the bottom, until the garlic is a paste.
2. Add the parsley, cilantro and the rest of the oil. Pulse, moving the head around, until it's a **coarse**
   purée, not smooth.
3. Taste for salt. Add lemon juice if you're serving it as a sauce rather than a marinade.

## To serve

As a marinade for fish or shrimp (an hour or so), or spooned over grilled fish, chicken, eggplant or roast
vegetables.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Up to 2 weeks tightly covered (per the NPR reprint of a Wolfert charmoula), with oil on top.
  Herbs will darken.
- **Freezer:** Untested. Herb-and-oil sauces usually freeze well in small portions. Test using the
  [freezer test protocol](../README.md#test-protocol).

## Notes

### From the source
- Blend to a **coarse** purée, on low speed.
- Wolfert writes that "there is no one recipe for charmoula." It changes by region and by what it's for;
  a Marrakech cook might add ginger.
- Her eggplant charmoula uses lemon juice instead of zest and a much smaller batch with a pinch of cayenne.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Quantities come from a third-party adaptation (PantryFlex) that credits the NPR reprint**, not from the
  NPR page or the book directly. The NPR article's quantities didn't come through in search. **Salt is an
  estimate**; check the book.
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** Garlic and spices go in first with some oil so the blade can make a paste
  before the herbs are added.
- **Gram estimates** are approximate. The source page couldn't be opened from this session; quantities came
  via search.
