# Immersion-Blender Hollandaise

Kenji López-Alt's stick-blender hollandaise: a yolk with a little lemon juice and water in the bottom of a
narrow cup, and very hot butter poured in while the blender runs. The hot butter cooks the yolk as it
emulsifies, so there's no double boiler and no whisking. It takes about two minutes.

## Ingredients

Double the source recipe, so there's enough depth to cover the blade in a quart deli container.

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 2 | Egg yolks | ~36 g. Source: 1 |
| ~10 g | Lemon juice | 2 teaspoons. Source: 1 teaspoon |
| ~10 g | Water | 2 teaspoons. Source: 1 teaspoon |
| ~226 g | Unsalted butter | 2 sticks (16 tablespoons). Source: 1 stick |
| | Kosher salt | A pinch, then to taste |
| | Cayenne or hot sauce | Optional, to taste |

## Method

1. Put the yolks, lemon juice, water and a pinch of salt in the bottom of a quart deli container. Set the
   stick blender head flat on the bottom over them.
2. Melt the butter in a small saucepan over high heat, swirling, until it's foaming hard and the foam starts
   to subside. **It should be about 220°F (104°C).** Pour it into a measuring cup with a spout.
3. Turn the blender on, keeping the head on the bottom. Pour the hot butter in a thin, steady stream. The
   sauce will thicken around the head as it goes. The whole pour should take about 30 seconds.
4. Once all the butter is in, slowly lift the head and push it back down a few times to finish. Season with
   salt and cayenne or hot sauce, and a few more drops of lemon if it needs it.

## To serve

Right away, on eggs Benedict, asparagus or fish. Hold it a short while in a warm (not hot) spot, such as a
thermos or a bowl over warm water.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Best used immediately. Leftovers keep 1–2 days and set solid; rewarm gently over warm water,
  whisking, with a splash of hot water (estimate).
- **Freezer:** Not recommended. Egg-butter emulsions break when frozen.

## Notes

### From the source
- **Use a cup that barely fits the blender head.** The yolk has to be under the blade.
- **The butter must be very hot**, about 220°F. That heat cooks the yolk; cooler butter makes a thin sauce.
- Pour slowly, about 30 seconds for the whole stick.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Doubled for the deli container.** A quart deli container is wider than the narrow cup the source calls
  for, and one yolk won't cover the blade. Two yolks and two sticks is the smallest batch to try; if the
  blade still isn't covered, tilt the container so the yolks pool around the head while you start the pour.
- **If it's thin or breaks:** put a fresh yolk and a teaspoon of hot water in a clean container, then blend
  the broken sauce back in slowly with the head on the bottom.
- Quantities (1 yolk, 1 teaspoon each lemon and water, 1 stick butter) are confirmed only through reposts and
  a separate recipe site that match each other. Check them against the Serious Eats original.
