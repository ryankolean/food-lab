# Toum (Lebanese Garlic Sauce)

The fluffy, bright-white garlic sauce served with shawarma and grilled chicken all over Lebanon. It's just
garlic, salt, oil and lemon, whipped into an emulsion with no egg. Maureen Abood is a Lebanese-American
cookbook author (*Rose Water & Orange Blossoms*) whose site is a go-to reference for Lebanese home cooking.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~40 g | Garlic | 8 cloves, peeled. Cut out any green germ |
| ~4 g | Kosher salt | 1 teaspoon |
| ~218 g | Neutral oil | 1 cup. Canola or similar; not olive oil |
| ~15 g | Lemon juice | 1 tablespoon, added a few drops at a time |
| 15–30 g | Ice-cold water | 1–2 tablespoons |

## Method

1. Put the garlic, salt and about 1 teaspoon of the lemon juice in a quart deli container. Tilt the container
   so everything pools on one side and blend with the stick blender, head pressed flat to the bottom, until
   the garlic is a completely smooth, sticky paste. Scrape down and repeat. This takes a few minutes; don't
   move on while there are still bits.
2. **Start the emulsion slowly.** With the blender running and the head on the bottom, add oil a teaspoon at
   a time, waiting until each addition disappears before adding the next. This first ¼ cup is where toum
   breaks.
3. Once the paste turns pale, thick and fluffy, add the oil in a thin stream. Every few tablespoons of oil,
   add a few drops of lemon juice and a splash of ice water. Slowly lift the head as the volume grows, then
   push it back down.
4. Stop when all the oil is in and the sauce is white, thick and spreadable. Taste for salt.

## To serve

With shawarma, chicken, grilled meats and fries. Very strong at first; it mellows over a day or two.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** About 2–4 weeks in a sealed container (estimate). The garlic flavor softens over time.
- **Freezer:** Not recommended. An eggless emulsion won't survive freezing.

## Notes

### From the source
- **Add the oil very slowly at first.** Abood says that when toum breaks, it's almost always because too
  much oil went in too fast.
- Alternate the oil with a few drops of lemon juice at a time until the toum is fluffy and spreadable.
- Abood writes that an immersion blender doesn't have enough blade surface to emulsify toum well and uses a
  food processor. Commenters on her page report success with a stick blender anyway.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** The tilted-container paste step and the head-on-the-bottom technique are not
  from the source. A small batch sits shallow in a quart deli container; if the blade isn't catching the
  garlic, **double the batch** (16 cloves, 2 cups oil, about 2½ cups finished), which still fits.
- **Don't use the "pour all the oil on top" mayonnaise trick here.** Without egg, toum needs the oil added
  gradually.
- **If it breaks:** in a clean container, blend 2 more cloves of garlic with a pinch of salt to a paste, then
  blend the broken sauce back in a spoonful at a time. Some Lebanese restaurants add an egg white as
  insurance; that makes it a different sauce, but it's a reliable fallback.
- **Gram estimates** for garlic, salt and oil are approximate. Fridge life is an estimate.
