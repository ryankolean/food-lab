# Rose Harissa

A smoky, sweet-hot North African chile paste with a floral finish from rose petals and rose water. Yotam
Ottolenghi made rose harissa a pantry staple in Britain and the US; this is his Test Kitchen's homemade
version from *Extra Good Things*, built on soaked dried Kashmiri and ancho chiles.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 40 g | Dried Kashmiri chiles | Stems and seeds discarded. Or another mild dried red chile (guajillo) |
| 25 g | Dried ancho chiles | Stems and seeds discarded |
| ~20 g | Garlic | 4 cloves, peeled. Charred, then chopped (Method step 1) |
| ~4 g | Cumin seeds | 2 teaspoons, roughly crushed |
| ~5 g | Coriander seeds | 1 tablespoon, roughly crushed |
| ~3 g | Caraway seeds | 1½ teaspoons, roughly crushed |
| ~4 g | Kashmiri chile powder or paprika | 1½ teaspoons |
| ~16 g | Tomato paste | 1 tablespoon |
| ~4 g | Flaked sea salt | 1½ teaspoons |
| ~6 g | Caster (superfine) sugar | 1½ teaspoons |
| ~30 g | Lemon juice | 2 tablespoons |
| ~60 g | Apple cider vinegar | 4 tablespoons |
| 120 ml (~110 g) | Olive oil | 8 tablespoons: 2 for the pan, 2 to blend, 4 stirred in at the end. Plus more to seal the jar |
| ~1–2 g | Dried rose petals | 1 tablespoon |
| ~10 g | Rose water | 2 teaspoons |

## Method

Ventilate the kitchen well; toasting the chiles throws off strong fumes.

1. Char the garlic cloves in a very hot, dry large frying pan for about 3 minutes, then chop roughly.
2. In the same pan, dry-toast the chiles for about a minute, until fragrant. Don't let them blacken.
3. Put them in a bowl, cover with boiling water, weigh them down with a small plate and soak 30 minutes.
   Drain (the source saves the soaking water for another use).
4. Put the pan back on medium-high heat with the crushed cumin, coriander and caraway, the chile powder, the
   tomato paste and 2 tablespoons of the oil. Cook, stirring, 2–3 minutes, until fragrant and deep red.
5. **Grind the crushed seeds finer for the stick blender.** Scrape the spice mixture into a mortar or spice
   grinder if the seeds are still in big pieces and break them down a bit more.
6. Tear the soaked chiles into pieces and put them in a quart deli container with the spice mixture, garlic,
   salt, sugar, rose petals, lemon juice, vinegar, rose water and 2 tablespoons of oil. Blend with the stick
   blender, head on the bottom, moving it around, to a **coarse** paste. Add a spoonful of water if the blade
   isn't catching.
7. Stir in the remaining 4 tablespoons of oil. It should stay coarse, not smooth. Taste for salt.
8. Pack into a sterilized jar and cover the surface with a layer of olive oil.

## To serve

Stirred into yogurt, rubbed on chicken or lamb before roasting, tossed with roast carrots, or spooned on eggs.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Up to 2 months, always sealed under oil (per source). Use a clean spoon each time and top up
  the oil.
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate: freeze in small portions. Test using the
  [freezer test protocol](../README.md#test-protocol).

## Notes

### From the source
- Char the garlic, toast and soak the chiles, and cook the crushed spices with the tomato paste and oil before
  blending everything to a **coarse** paste.
- Any mild dried red chile can stand in for the Kashmiri chiles.
- The recipe makes about 375 g, which mostly fills a 1 lb jar.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- Kashmiri chiles are prized for their red color with mild heat; guajillo is a good stand-in.
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** The source pulses everything in a food processor. A stick blender won't break
  down crushed seeds well, so grind them finer after cooking (step 5). Soaked chiles are fine; blend longer
  than feels necessary.
- **Gram estimates** for the spices, garlic, salt and rose petals are approximate.
