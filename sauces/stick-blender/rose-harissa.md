# Rose Harissa

A smoky, sweet-hot North African chile paste with a floral finish from rose petals and rose water. Yotam
Ottolenghi made rose harissa a pantry staple in Britain and the US; this is his Test Kitchen's homemade
version from *Extra Good Things*, built on soaked dried Kashmiri and ancho chiles.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 40 g | Dried Kashmiri chiles | Stems and seeds discarded. Or another mild dried red chile (guajillo) |
| 25 g | Dried ancho chiles | Stems and seeds discarded |
| ~20 g | Garlic | 4 cloves, peeled |
| ~4 g | Cumin seeds | 2 teaspoons |
| ~5 g | Coriander seeds | 1 tablespoon |
| ~3 g | Caraway seeds | 1½ teaspoons |
| ~4 g | Kashmiri chile powder or paprika | 1½ teaspoons |
| ~16 g | Tomato paste | 1 tablespoon |
| ~6 g | Salt | 1½ teaspoons. **From a different reprint; see Notes** |
| ~6 g | Sugar | 1½ teaspoons. **From a different reprint** |
| ~30 g | Lemon juice | 2 tablespoons. **From a different reprint** |
| ~60 g | Cider vinegar | 4 tablespoons. **From a different reprint** |
| 120 ml (~110 g) | Olive oil | Plus more to seal the jar |
| ~1–2 g | Dried rose petals | 1 tablespoon |
| ~10 g | Rose water | 2 teaspoons |

## Method

1. Toast the chiles in a hot dry pan, a few seconds per side, until fragrant. Don't let them blacken.
2. Put them in a bowl, cover with boiling water and soak 20–30 minutes, until completely soft. Drain, keeping
   a little of the water.
3. Meanwhile, toast the cumin, coriander and caraway in the same pan until fragrant, then grind them fine in a
   spice grinder or mortar.
4. Tear the soaked chiles into pieces and put them in a quart deli container with the garlic, ground spices,
   chile powder, tomato paste, salt, sugar, lemon juice, vinegar and half the oil. Blend with the stick
   blender, head on the bottom, moving it around, until it's a fairly smooth paste. Add a spoonful of soaking
   water if the blade isn't catching.
5. Blend in the rest of the oil, then the rose petals and rose water. Taste for salt.
6. Pack into a jar and cover the surface with a thin layer of olive oil.

## To serve

Stirred into yogurt, rubbed on chicken or lamb before roasting, tossed with roast carrots, or spooned on eggs.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Several weeks under a layer of oil (estimate). Top up the oil after each use.
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate: freeze in small portions. Test using the
  [freezer test protocol](../README.md#test-protocol).

## Notes

### From the source
- Toast the chiles, soak them in boiling water, then blend everything to a **coarse** paste.
- Kashmiri chiles give color with mild heat; any mild dried red chile works.
- The recipe makes 375 g.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Salt, sugar, lemon and vinegar amounts** are not in the Happy Foodie text found through search; they come
  from the MasterClass and Cooked reprints of Ottolenghi's rose harissa, which may be a different version
  (MasterClass also uses 1 tablespoon of rose water instead of 2 teaspoons). Check against the book.
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** A stick blender won't break down whole seeds, so grind them first (or use
  ground spices). Soaked chiles are fine; blend longer than feels necessary and expect a slightly coarser
  paste than a food processor gives.
- **Gram estimates** for the spices, garlic and rose petals are approximate.
- The source page couldn't be opened from this session; quantities came via search.
