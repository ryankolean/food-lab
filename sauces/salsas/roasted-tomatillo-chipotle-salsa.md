# Roasted Tomatillo Salsa with Chipotle and Roasted Garlic

*Salsa de tomatillo asado con chipotle.* From Roberto Santibañez, chef of Fonda in New York and author of
*Truly Mexican*, a book built around salsas. He uses it to show his main rule: **toasting the dry ingredients
is what gives a salsa depth.** Smoky chipotle mora, sweet roasted garlic and charred tomatillo.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~227 g | Tomatillos | ½ lb (about 5), husked and rinsed. **Double this**; see Notes |
| 3 | Dried chipotle mora (morita) chiles | Stemmed; ~9 g. Or 6 dried árbol chiles |
| 6 cloves | Garlic | Unpeeled; ~30 g |
| | Water | Amount not confirmed; start with ~60 g (¼ cup) |
| ~3 g | Salt | ½ teaspoon, to taste |

## Method

1. Heat a comal or heavy skillet over medium-low heat. Toast the chiles and roast the garlic, turning often,
   until the chiles are puffed and blistered in spots (3–5 minutes) and the garlic is tender and golden brown
   with blackened spots (8–10 minutes). Take each off as it's done.
2. Peel the garlic. Soak the chiles in hot water for 20 minutes (stick-blender step; see Notes).
3. Roast the tomatillos on the comal or under the broiler, turning, until blackened in spots and soft
   through.
4. Put the tomatillos, drained chiles, garlic, water and salt into a tall container. Blend with the stick
   blender until smooth. The tomatillo seeds will still be visible.
5. Thin with more water if needed and adjust the salt.

## To serve

Santibañez suggests spooning it over grilled skirt steak or tacos, or serving it with a bowl of chips.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Up to 5 days (per source).
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate.

## Notes

### From the source
- Dry-roast, no oil: this is how it's traditionally done in Mexico, Santibañez says.
- The tomatillo seeds will still be visible after blending. That's expected.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Yield warning:** readers report that ½ lb tomatillos made only about ¼ cup of salsa, not the ½ cup the
  recipe states. **Start with 1 lb tomatillos** and scale the chiles and garlic to taste.
- **Water amount** wasn't in the search results. ¼ cup is a guess; one republished copy also lists
  "12 lb tomatillos," which is a typo for ½ lb.
- Soaking the chiles is added for the stick blender.
- **Gram estimates** are approximate. The recipe was found through search; the source pages couldn't be
  opened from this session.
