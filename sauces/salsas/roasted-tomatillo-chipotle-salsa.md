# Roasted Tomatillo Salsa with Chipotle and Roasted Garlic

*Salsa de tomatillo asado con chipotle.* From Roberto Santibañez, chef of Fonda in New York and author of
*Truly Mexican*, a book built around salsas. Tasting Table presents it as a lesson in how **toasting the dry
ingredients gives a salsa depth** and serious spice. Smoky chipotle mora, sweet roasted garlic and charred
tomatillo.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~227 g | Tomatillos | ½ lb (about 5), husked and rinsed |
| 3 | Dried chipotle mora (morita) chiles | Wiped clean and stemmed; ~9 g. Or 6 dried árbol chiles |
| 6 cloves | Garlic | Unpeeled; ~30 g |
| ~120 g | Water | ½ cup |
| ~3 g | Kosher salt | ½ teaspoon, to taste |

## Method

1. Heat the broiler with a rack about 8 in from the heat. Broil the tomatillos on a small foil-lined baking
   sheet, turning once, until blackened and cooked through, about 20 minutes. Let them cool to room
   temperature.
2. Meanwhile, warm a cast iron skillet over medium-low heat. Toast the chiles, turning often, until puffed and
   blistered, about 4 minutes, and take them off. Keep roasting the garlic, turning, until tender and golden
   with some blackened spots, about 8 minutes more. Cool slightly and peel.
3. Soak the chiles in hot water for 20 minutes (stick-blender step; see Notes), then drain.
4. Put the tomatillos, chiles, garlic, water and ½ teaspoon salt into a quart deli container. Blend with the
   stick blender until smooth. The tomatillo seeds will still be visible.
5. Adjust the salt.

## To serve

Santibañez suggests spooning it over grilled skirt steak or tacos, or serving it with tortilla chips.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Up to 5 days in an airtight container (per source).
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate.

## Notes

### From the source
- The tomatillo seeds will still be visible after blending. That's expected.
