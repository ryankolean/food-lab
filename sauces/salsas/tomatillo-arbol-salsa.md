# Tomatillo-Árbol Chile Salsa

Bayless's fiery red-brown salsa: sixteen toasted árbol chiles, a whole head's worth of roasted garlic and a
pound of broiled tomatillos. It's very spicy, very garlicky and tart. Bayless built Frontera Grill and his
cookbooks on salsas like this one.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~16 | Dried chiles de árbol | Stemmed; ~8 g |
| 6 cloves | Garlic | Unpeeled; ~30 g |
| ~454 g | Tomatillos | 1 lb, husked and rinsed |
| ~60 g | Water | About ¼ cup, to thin |
| ~5 g | Salt | A scant teaspoon |
| ½ teaspoon | Sugar | Optional; ~2 g |

## Method

1. In an ungreased skillet over medium heat, toast the chiles, stirring, until very aromatic and a shade
   darker, about 1 minute. Don't let them smoke. Cover them with hot tap water and soak 30 minutes, keeping
   them submerged.
2. In the same skillet, roast the unpeeled garlic, turning, until soft and blackened in spots, about
   15 minutes. Cool and peel.
3. Heat the broiler. Put the tomatillos on a baking sheet and broil about 4 in from the heat until darkly
   roasted and soft, about 5 minutes per side. Cool slightly and keep all the juices.
4. Drain the chiles. Put the tomatillos and their juices, the chiles and the garlic in a tall container and
   blend smooth with the stick blender.
5. Stir in enough water to make it spoonable, usually about ¼ cup. Season with salt and the sugar if you're
   using it (it softens the tomatillo tartness).

## To serve

With tacos, carne asada or chips. It's very hot, so use it sparingly.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** About 5 days (estimate).
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate.

## Notes

### From the source
- The sugar is optional and only there to balance tart tomatillos.
- Bayless also has a red-tomato version, *Roasted Tomato-Árbol Chile Salsa*: 12 árbol chiles, 4 garlic
  cloves and 12 oz ripe tomatoes instead of tomatillos, made the same way. It's worth testing next to this
  one.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick blender:** the 30-minute soak in the source already makes this work. Blend for a full minute or
  more to break down the chile skins.
- **Gram estimates** for the chiles, garlic and sugar are approximate, not from the source.
- The recipe was found through search; rickbayless.com couldn't be opened from this session.
