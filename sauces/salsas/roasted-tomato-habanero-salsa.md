# Roasted Tomato-Habanero Salsa (Chiltomate)

Chiltomate is the Yucatán's everyday table salsa: broiled tomatoes, a broiled habanero for its fruity heat,
and blackened onion. Bayless describes it as the Yucatecan counterpart to central Mexico's roasted-tomato
molcajete salsa. It's very simple and tastes strongly of char.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| ~454 g | Ripe tomatoes | 1 lb: about 2 medium-large round or 6–8 plum |
| 1–2 | Fresh habanero chiles | Stemmed; ~8 g each. 1 for medium-hot, 2 for hot |
| 1 small | White onion | Sliced ¼ in thick; ~100 g |
| ~8 g | Cilantro | About ½ cup loosely packed, chopped |
| ~5 g | Salt | About 1 teaspoon |
| 1–2 tablespoons | Water | Only if too thick |

## Method

1. Heat the broiler with the rack 4 in below it. Put the whole tomatoes, habanero(s) and sliced onion on a
   rimmed baking sheet. Broil until blackened on top, about 5–6 minutes.
2. Take the onion and habaneros off. Flip the tomatoes and broil the other side until blackened and soft,
   about 5 minutes more. Let everything cool a bit.
3. *(Stick-blender version; see Notes.)* Put the tomatoes and their juices, the habanero(s), the roasted
   onion, the cilantro and the salt into a tall container. Blend smooth with the stick blender.
4. Thin with water a tablespoon at a time if needed. Taste for salt.

## To serve

At room temperature. The classic pairing is cochinita pibil or poc chuc, and it's also very good on eggs.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** About 5 days (estimate).
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate.

## Notes

### From the source
- Bayless's original is a **coarse** salsa: the tomatoes and habanero are pulsed to a rough purée, and the
  broiled onion is chopped finely and stirred in with the cilantro.
- Bayless also has a cooked version, the "Essential Simmered Tomato-Habanero Sauce," which is the Yucatecan
  way to serve it as a room-temperature sauce.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick-blender adaptation:** to fit the no-knife, smooth brief, the roasted onion and cilantro are blended
  in rather than chopped and stirred in. That makes it smoother and slightly sweeter. Try both.
- **Habanero handling:** wear gloves when stemming roasted habaneros. Start with one and taste before adding
  the second.
- **Gram estimates** for the onion, cilantro and salt are approximate, not from the source.
