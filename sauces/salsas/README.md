# Salsas: Smooth, Stick-Blender Salsas

Salsas with a lot of flavor and very little knife work. Every recipe here follows the same rule: **the only
prep is cleaning, charring or toasting the ingredients, then everything is blended smooth with a stick
(immersion) blender in one quart (32 oz) deli container.** No dicing and no pico de gallo. These are smooth,
spoonable, taqueria-style table salsas, and the deli container they're blended in is the one they're stored in.

## The method: shared rules for every salsa here

These come from the sources (Santibañez, Bayless and Jinich all teach versions of them) plus what a stick
blender needs.

### Building flavor

1. **Char hard.** Dry-roast tomatoes and tomatillos on a comal, cast iron or under the broiler until blackened
   in spots and fully collapsed. The black spots are the flavor. Don't use oil when dry-roasting, which is
   the traditional Mexican method.
2. **Roast garlic in its skin**, on the same pan, until soft and blackened in spots (10–15 min). Peel it once
   it's cool. Raw garlic gets harsh in a blended salsa; roasted garlic turns sweet.
3. **Toast dried chiles briefly.** Seconds per side, just until fragrant and puffed. A burnt chile makes the
   whole batch bitter, so if one smokes or blackens, throw it out.
4. **Season after blending, then rest.** Salt, taste, rest 15–30 minutes, and taste again. Most smooth salsas
   need more salt than you'd expect, and lime or a pinch of sugar can fix a flat or too-sour batch.

### Blending with a stick blender

5. **Soak dried chiles.** A stick blender can't break down dry chile skins like a high-powered jar blender
   can. Soak toasted dried chiles in hot water for 20–30 minutes before blending, even where the source
   skips this step, and use some of the soaking water as the blending liquid.
6. **Blend in a quart deli container.** It's tall and narrow enough for the blade to catch everything, and the salsa can be stored in it. Put the soft,
   watery things (tomatoes, tomatillos, liquid) in first and the chiles and garlic on top.
7. **Keep the blade head submerged.** Lifting it pulls in air and turns a red salsa frothy and pink. Pulse to
   break things down, then run it steadily while moving the head slowly up and down.
8. **Let hot ingredients cool a little first.** Blending boiling-hot vegetables splatters; warm is fine.
9. **Thin last.** Add water a tablespoon at a time until it's spoonable.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** most cooked salsas keep about 5 days. Avocado and sour cream salsas are best within a few hours
  and keep 1–2 days at most.
- **Freezer:** untested. Cooked tomato and tomatillo salsas without avocado or dairy should freeze reasonably
  well (re-blend after thawing to bring the texture back). Avocado and sour cream versions won't. Test using
  the [freezer test protocol](../README.md#test-protocol).
