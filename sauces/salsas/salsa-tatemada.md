# Salsa Tatemada

*Tatemado* means charred. This is the smoky charred salsa served at Contramar, Gabriela Cámara's famous Mexico
City seafood restaurant, alongside its *pescado a la talla* (grilled whole snapper). "The whole point of
this sauce is to get the flavor of the charring," Cámara says. Every ingredient is blistered on a hot comal,
then ground together. Fresh serranos give it brightness, and dried morita chiles add smoke.

## Ingredients

> **Quantities not confirmed.** Cámara's exact amounts couldn't be retrieved. The quantities below follow
> a published adaptation of her MasterClass recipe plus the morita that her version uses. Treat them as a
> starting point.

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 300–350 g | Tomatoes | Adaptation used cherry tomatoes; Romas work too |
| 1 large | White onion | Cut into 5–7 wedges; ~250 g |
| 5 cloves | Garlic | Unpeeled; ~25 g |
| 1–2 | Fresh serrano chiles | ~6 g each. Jalapeño is the source's suggested swap |
| 1–2 | Dried morita chiles | ~3 g each. Dried chipotle is the source's suggested swap |
| ~5 g | Salt | To taste |
| | Water | Only to thin |

## Method

1. Heat a dry comal or cast iron skillet over medium-high heat. Blister the tomatoes, onion wedges, unpeeled
   garlic and serranos, turning, until deeply blackened in spots and softened. Take each one off as it's
   done. The garlic will take the longest.
2. Toast the moritas briefly, a few seconds per side, until fragrant and puffed. Soak them in hot water for
   20 minutes (stick-blender step; see Notes).
3. Peel the garlic. Stem the serranos.
4. Put everything in a tall container with the salt and blend with the stick blender. Pulse for a rustic
   texture or run longer for smooth. Thin with water or chile soaking water. Taste for salt.

## To serve

With grilled whole fish (the Contramar way), steak, tacos or eggs.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** About 5 days (estimate).
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate.

## Notes

### From the source
- Cámara grinds this in a **molcajete** on purpose, for a rustic handmade texture. The stick blender is a
  shortcut; pulse it for something close.
- The chiles are fresh serranos plus their dried, smoked relative, the morita. Her suggested swaps are
  jalapeño for serrano and dried chipotle for morita.
- At Contramar it's served with *pescado a la talla*, next to a raw *salsa verde cruda*.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **All quantities** come from a third-party adaptation (cookedandloved.com) that roasts in the oven, not
  on a comal. The morita count is a guess. Check against *My Mexico City Kitchen* or the MasterClass page.
- Soaking the morita is added for the stick blender.
