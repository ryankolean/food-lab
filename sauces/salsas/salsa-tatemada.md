# Salsa Tatemada

*Tatemado* means charred. This is the smoky charred salsa that Contramar, Gabriela Cámara's Mexico City
restaurant, serves with its *pescado a la talla* (grilled whole snapper), which she calls the restaurant's
most popular dish. "The whole point of this sauce is to get the flavor of the charring," Cámara says. Dried
moritas, garlic, onion, serranos, tomatillos and plum tomatoes are all charred on a very hot comal, then
ground together with salt and cilantro. Fresh serranos give it brightness, and the morita adds smoke.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 8 g | Dried morita chiles | Destemmed. Dried chipotle is the source's suggested swap |
| 20 g | Garlic | Roughly chopped |
| 150 g | White onion | Roughly chopped |
| 12 g | Fresh serrano chiles | Sliced in half lengthwise and deseeded. Jalapeño is the source's suggested swap |
| 200 g | Tomatillos | Husks discarded |
| 530 g | Plum tomatoes | Halved, stems removed |
| | Salt | To taste |
| 14 g | Cilantro | |

## Method

1. Heat a dry comal or cast iron skillet over high heat. Open a window and turn on the fan. Char the moritas
   and garlic until the chiles soften slightly, 2–3 minutes.
2. Take the moritas off and soak them in warm water for 10–15 minutes, until soft enough to grind. Leave the
   garlic blistering and add the onion.
3. After 3–4 minutes, take the garlic off. Keep charring the onion, then add the serranos cut side down, and
   the tomatillos and tomatoes flesh side down. Turn the serranos so both sides blister. Keep roasting and
   flipping everything for 10–15 minutes, until charred and cooked through. Turn off the heat and leave the
   vegetables on the surface.
4. Put all the roasted ingredients, the drained moritas, salt and cilantro in a quart deli container. Pulse
   with the stick blender until chunky, or run it longer for smooth. Taste for salt and serve right away.

## To serve

With grilled whole fish (the Contramar way), tacos, chips, birria, quesadillas, burritos or roasted
vegetables.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Up to 4 days, covered (per source).
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate.

## Notes

### From the source
- The traditional tools are a comal and a **molcajete**; the source gives a cast iron skillet and a blender
  as substitutes. In the molcajete, grind the onion, one tomatillo and the garlic to a rough paste first, then
  the remaining tomatillos, tomatoes and salt, picking out any large tomato skins. Add the softened chiles and
  grind until their skins break down, then stir the cilantro in gently without mashing it.
- For less heat, remove the veins of the morita; they hold more heat than the seeds.
- The chiles are fresh serranos plus their dried, smoked relative, the morita. Her suggested swaps are
  jalapeño for serrano and dried chipotle for morita.
- The ingredient list says the serranos are deseeded, but the method says to char them "with seeds intact."
  Pick one and log it.
