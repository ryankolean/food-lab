# Salsa Roja with Oregano and Chile de Árbol

The simplest salsa in this collection and probably the best place to start. Tomatoes are charred until
they collapse, then blended whole (skins, cores, seeds and all) with quickly toasted árbol chiles, a lot of
toasted dried oregano and one garlic clove. Five ingredients and no knife work.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 680 g | Ripe Roma tomatoes | 1½ lb. Left whole: not peeled, not cored |
| 4–6 | Dried chiles de árbol | Stemmed, seeds left in; ~2–3 g. 4 for medium, 6 for hot |
| 1 tablespoon | Dried oregano | ~2–3 g. Mexican oregano if you have it |
| 1 clove | Garlic | Peeled; ~5 g |
| 1 teaspoon | Kosher or coarse sea salt | ~5 g, then to taste |

## Method

1. Heat a comal, cast iron skillet or heavy pan over medium heat. Char the whole tomatoes, turning often, for
   15–20 minutes, until very soft and charred on all sides. Or broil them for 10–12 minutes.
2. Put the tomatoes (skins, cores and seeds left on) into a quart deli container.
3. In the same pan, toast the chiles for a couple of minutes, turning, until browned and fragrant. Don't let
   them burn. Lower the heat and toast the oregano for only 5–20 seconds, stirring constantly; take it off
   right away.
4. Add the chiles with their seeds, the oregano, garlic and salt to the tomatoes. Blend smooth with the stick
   blender, keeping the head submerged. Taste for salt.

## To serve

Room temperature, with tacos, eggs, chips or anything grilled.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** Up to 5 days, covered (per source).
- **Freezer:** Untested. A cooked tomato salsa with no avocado or dairy, so it's a good freezer candidate.

## Notes

### From the source
- The chiles go in **with their seeds**. The heat comes from leaving the seeds in.
- The chile and oregano toasting step is for the comal/skillet route; toast the chiles until browned and
  fragrant, and the oregano for seconds only, so neither burns.
- The tomatoes go in whole: unpeeled, uncored and with seeds. The charred skin is part of the flavor.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** The source uses a jar blender. Dried árbol chiles may not break down fully
  with a stick blender. If flecks of chile skin are left, try soaking the toasted chiles in hot water for
  15 minutes first, or blend the chiles, garlic and oregano with a few tomatoes first and add the rest after.
- **Gram estimates** for chiles, oregano, garlic and salt are approximate, not from the source.
