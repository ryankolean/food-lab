# Salsa Roja Taquera (Red Taco-Stand Salsa)

The thin, brick-red, very hot salsa found at Mexico City taco stands. Toasted árbol chiles give the heat,
guajillo gives the color and a fruity depth, and charred plum tomatoes and roasted garlic hold it together.
This is the *Eat Mexico* book version; Téllez's earlier blog version is in Notes.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 8–10 | Dried chiles de árbol | Stems snipped, seeds shaken out (keep them for hotter); ~4–5 g |
| 2 | Dried guajillo chiles | Stems snipped, seeds shaken out; ~12 g |
| 3 medium cloves | Garlic | Unpeeled; ~15 g |
| 2 | Ripe plum (Roma) tomatoes | ~200 g |
| ~30 g | Chile soaking water | 2 tablespoons, reserved from the soak |
| ~90 g | Water | A generous ¼ cup, then 2 tablespoons more. Add more to thin |
| ~4 g | Salt | ¾ teaspoon, then to taste |

## Method

1. Snip the stems off the chiles and shake out as many seeds as you can. If they're brittle, warm them
   briefly on a gently heated comal first.
2. Roast the unpeeled garlic on a comal or nonstick skillet over low to medium-low heat, **near the edge of
   the pan**, turning occasionally, until soft and blackened in spots, 5–7 minutes. Let it cool, peel it and
   put it in a quart deli container.
3. Toast the chiles **one at a time** near the edge of the pan, turning constantly, 5–10 seconds each, until
   they lighten in spots and smell spicy. Put them in a bowl, cover with water and soak 15 minutes until the
   skins soften. Keep the soaking water.
4. Raise the heat to medium or medium-high. Roast the whole tomatoes, turning with tongs, until soft and
   blackened in spots.
5. Add the drained chiles, 2 tablespoons of the soaking water and a generous ¼ cup water to the garlic. Blend
   smooth with the stick blender, keeping the head submerged so it doesn't foam.
6. Add the tomatoes, 2 more tablespoons water and ¾ teaspoon salt, and blend again until completely smooth.
   The finished salsa should be pourable, not thick. Adjust the water and salt; it tastes less salty as it
   cools.

## To serve

At room temperature, spooned thin over tacos, especially pork. A little goes a long way.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** About 5 days (estimate; not stated in the sources).
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate: no avocado or dairy.

## Notes

### From the source
- **Use real guajillos.** Chiles colorados are sometimes sold labeled as guajillos in the US. The book warns
  that "the thinner-skinned colorados will make the salsa taste too sweet," and the color won't be as deep.
- Toast the chiles one at a time so none of them burn.
- **Blog version (2011):** 8 árbol (seeds removed, reserved and added back to the blender; the original
  called for 10), 2 guajillos seeded and deveined, 3 Romas (300 g / 11 oz), 3 garlic cloves **peeled and
  roughly chopped, raw**, ⅓ cup plain water, salt (about 1 teaspoon). The chiles are toasted a few seconds
  and soaked in hot water about 20 minutes, and the blog warns that blackening the chiles makes the salsa
  bitter.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** The soak in step 3 already suits a stick blender. Blend for longer than feels
  necessary: guajillo skins are tough. Optionally pass the salsa through a sieve for a perfectly smooth
  taqueria texture.
- **Gram estimates** for the chiles, garlic, tomatoes, water and salt are approximate, not from the source.
