# Salsa Roja Taquera (Red Taco-Stand Salsa)

The thin, brick-red, very hot salsa found at Mexico City taco stands. Toasted árbol chiles give the heat,
guajillo gives the color and a fruity depth, and charred plum tomatoes and roasted garlic hold it together.
Téllez wrote *Eat Mexico* after years of leading street-food tours in Mexico City.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Notes |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 8–10 | Dried chiles de árbol | Stemmed; ~4–5 g |
| 2 | Dried guajillo chiles | Stemmed, seeded and deveined; ~12 g |
| 3 medium cloves | Garlic | Unpeeled; ~15 g |
| 2–3 | Ripe plum (Roma) tomatoes | ~300 g. Book says 2; blog says 3 (300 g) |
| ~80 g | Chile soaking water | ⅓ cup (blog version). Add more to thin |
| ~5 g | Salt | About 1 teaspoon, to taste |

## Method

1. Heat a comal or heavy skillet over medium heat. Toast the garlic **away from the hottest spot**, turning,
   until soft and blackened in spots. Let it cool and peel it.
2. Toast the chiles **one at a time**, a few seconds per side, just until fragrant. Don't let them blacken.
3. Cover the toasted chiles with hot water and soak about 20 minutes. Keep the soaking water.
4. Meanwhile, char the tomatoes on the comal (or under the broiler), turning, until soft and blackened in
   spots.
5. Put the tomatoes, drained chiles, peeled garlic, ⅓ cup of the soaking water and salt into a quart deli
   container. Blend completely smooth with the stick blender, keeping the head submerged so it doesn't foam.
6. Thin with more soaking water if needed. The finished salsa should be pourable, not thick. Season with
   salt.

## To serve

Spooned thin over tacos, especially pork. A little goes a long way.

## Storage

- **Fridge:** About 5 days (estimate; not stated in the sources found).
- **Freezer:** Untested. A good candidate: no avocado or dairy.

## Notes

### From the source
- **Use árbol and guajillo specifically.** The book warns against swapping in thinner-skinned *chiles
  colorados*, which make the salsa taste too sweet.
- Toast the chiles one at a time so none of them burn.
- There are two versions: the book (8–10 árbol, 2 tomatoes) and Téllez's 2011 blog (8 árbol, 3 Romas /
  300 g, ⅓ cup water). The table above combines them; the blog version is the more precise of the two.

### Added later (not in the source, confirm or edit)
- **Stick-blender adaptation.** The soak in step 3 already suits a stick blender. Blend for longer than feels
  necessary: guajillo skins are tough. Optionally pass the salsa through a sieve for a perfectly smooth
  taqueria texture.
- **Gram estimates** for chiles and garlic are approximate, not from the source.
- The quantities were found through search; the recipe pages couldn't be opened from this session.
